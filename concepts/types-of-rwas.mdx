When you open [Discover](/app-guide/basic/discover), you'll see assets split into three groups: **Stablecoins**, **Sovereign Bonds**, and **Native**. Here's what each group actually means, and what's in it today.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Sovereign Bonds">
    A **bond** is one of the oldest ways to invest money: a government needs to borrow money to pay for things, so it asks people to lend it some. In exchange, the government promises to pay that money back later, plus a bit extra as a thank-you (that "extra" is the return you earn).

    A **sovereign bond** just means the government itself is the one borrowing (as opposed to a company). It's normally something you'd need a bank or broker to buy for you. On Neko, that same investment shows up as a digital token you can buy directly, and it slowly grows in value over time to reflect that "extra" the government pays.

    These are Sovereign Bonds tokens on Neko, they pay you a return just for holding them. See [APY](/concepts/apy) for how that return is shown to you.

          **Mexican Treasury Token**

          Represents money lent to the Mexican government through CETES (Certificados de la Tesorería), one of the most common short-term investments Mexican banks themselves use. The government typically pays this debt back within 28 to 364 days, and the token's price quietly ticks up every day to reflect the interest building up, so you don't have to claim anything manually.

          **Issuer:** Etherfuse, a fintech company that builds tokenized versions of Latin American government debt. Etherfuse's CETES token is cleared by Mexico's banking and securities regulator (CNBV) for sale to everyday retail investors, not just large institutions, which is what makes it legal to offer to the general public rather than a workaround of the rules.

          **US Treasury Token**

          Represents money lent to the US government through short-term US Treasury Bills, widely considered one of the safest investments in the world because the US government has never failed to pay this kind of debt back. Like CETES, the price accrues daily to reflect the interest earned.

          **Issuer:** Etherfuse, a fintech company that builds tokenized versions of Latin American government debt. Etherfuse's USTRY token is cleared by Mexico's banking and securities regulator (CNBV) for sale to everyday retail investors, not just large institutions, which is what makes it legal to offer to the general public rather than a workaround of the rules.

          Represents money lent to the Brazilian government through Tesouro Direto, Brazil's official retail government bond program, the same one millions of Brazilians already use through their banks and brokers.

          **Issuer:** Etherfuse, a fintech company that builds tokenized versions of Latin American government debt.   <Tab title="Stablecoins">
    A **stablecoin** is a digital token built to always be worth the same as a real-world currency, usually one US dollar. It doesn't go up or down in value like other crypto, it's meant to just sit there and hold its value, the same way a dollar bill in your pocket does.

    People use stablecoins to hold their money safely between other actions, or to pay for something (like buying a bond token above). On their own, four of the five below don't earn you anything, they just hold their value. USDY is the exception.

          **USD Coin**

          Always worth about one US dollar. It's one of the most widely used stablecoins anywhere, accepted across almost every crypto app and exchange.

          **Issuer:** Circle, a US-regulated financial company that holds one real dollar (in cash or short-term US government debt) for every USDC in circulation, and publishes monthly reports confirming those reserves so anyone can check.

          Always worth about one euro, the same idea as USDC but for the eurozone.

          **Issuer:** Circle, the same company behind USDC, holding real euro reserves for every EURC in circulation and publishing the same kind of monthly reports.

          **US Dollar Yieldcoin**

          Tracks the US dollar and slowly grows in value on top of that, so Discover labels it "Yield Stablecoin" to make the difference obvious.

          **Issuer:** Ondo Finance, a company focused on bringing US Treasury yield on-chain. USDY is backed by short-term US Treasuries and bank deposits, and its value accrues daily as that underlying debt pays interest, similar to how the bond tokens above work.

  <Tab title="Native">
    Every blockchain (the digital network that Neko runs on) has its own built-in currency that keeps the network running, similar to how a toll road needs coins to pay the toll. That built-in currency is what "Native" means here: it belongs to the network itself, not to a government or a company.

    Unlike a bond, XLM's value isn't tied to any government's promise to pay you back, it moves based on how much people want to use and hold it. Today it's the only asset in this group, but "Native" is a category, not just one coin. If Neko ever adds support for other blockchains, each of those would bring its own native currency into this same group.

          **Stellar Lumens**

          The built-in currency of the Stellar network, which Neko runs on today. You need a tiny amount of it in your wallet at all times, it's what pays the small fee every time you do something on Neko, like buying an asset or moving money. Stellar has been running since 2014 and is used by payment companies and financial institutions worldwide, not just by Neko.

          **Backed by:** the Stellar network itself, and supported by the Stellar Development Foundation, a nonprofit that funds and maintains the network's software.
        </div>
      </Card>
